The script bench · stages 2 and 3

Fourteen passes, in order

Nothing here writes a whole episode in one shot. Each pass takes the previous script and changes one thing about it — structure, then voice, then truth, then performance. The order matters: naturalness runs before fact-checking, so corrections land in already-natural lines rather than re-flattening them.

Two small models sit off to the side: Haiku 4.5 writes the episode title and the ten-word MusicGen prompt. And after the script is final, another Sonnet pass updates host_memory.json and personal_context.json — which is how episode 40 can refer back to episode 12.

The only closed loop

A listener who doesn't already know the answer

Everything else in the chain is a straight line. This is the one place the script can be sent backwards. Two synthetic readers go through the draft — one naive, reading turn by turn and flagging where it lost the thread; one expert, looking for spots that sound substantive but say nothing. Their findings are turned into specific repair moves and the draft is re-read.

Stage 4 · audio

Rendered per turn, arranged as one program

Text-to-speech happens a turn at a time, in parallel, each speaker on their own routed voice and provider. That leaves a pile of clips with no timing — so the arrangement stage is where the conversation actually becomes conversational. Each turn's baked-in head and tail silence is trimmed off first, so the inserted gap is the perceived gap; then the gap flexes by context rather than sitting flat at 180 ms everywhere.

One voice per speaker, one provider per voice

Each speaker label resolves to its own route: a provider, a voice id and a model. Juno and Caspar sit on OpenAI's gpt-4o-mini-tts by default, which is the only model that accepts the per-line emotion tags the script carries as spoken-word instructions. A guest can be routed across to ElevenLabs or Cartesia so a third voice doesn't sound like a recolored host.

What stops a run, and what doesn't

Almost nothing is allowed to block publication

The design bias is loud: an episode that ships slightly diminished beats an episode that doesn't ship. Only a handful of conditions actually halt a run, and every one of them means the output would be worthless rather than merely worse. Everything else degrades, records a warning on the run manifest, and keeps going.

Halts

More than 20% of turns fail to render.

Usually a provider auth or quota wall hitting every turn at once — the alternative is publishing a near-silent episode.

Halts

A digest run finds no candidate papers.

Aborts before the ledger is touched, so nothing gets marked covered.

Halts

No ANTHROPIC_API_KEY on a digest run.

Both the ranker and the script build need it.

Degrades

Clip extraction fails → dialogue-only audio.

Degrades

A sonic cue can't be resolved or licensed → ship without it.

Cues are also dropped when clips are on; the two don't co-mix yet.

Degrades

MusicGen or audiocraft unavailable → a numpy ambient pad, or just the show ident.

Degrades

Mastering or duration probe fails → publish the premaster, note it on the manifest.

Degrades

The comprehension gate can't clear in two rounds → publish, and save the trace.

Degrades

The ledger write after a successful digest fails → warn only.

Bookkeeping never blocks a shipped episode.

What the run leaves behind

The work directory — per-turn MP3s, downloaded cues, music stems, the intermediate scripts — is deleted when the run exits. Five things survive next to the audio, and they are what any later editorial review actually reads:

  • .script.txt — the final spoken script, for the flow and AI-tell passes
  • .chapters.json — chapter marks derived from the beat sheet
  • .companion.json — sources, claims, follow-up links, credits
  • .listener_trace.json — what the synthetic listeners flagged, and what survived repair
  • .story_spine.json — the backbone the episode was built from

A durable copy of the run manifest is written alongside them, whether the run succeeded or failed — so a crashed run still says which stage it died in.
