JUNO [cold open]: This week's lead paper gives CEM screening programs a number they've been missing — what incidence-round detection rates actually look like after the prevalence screen has run. The first-round yield is not the steady-state yield, and now there's data to say so.
CASPAR [precise]: From Radiology, 2026: Lee and colleagues, a multicenter study of nearly 7,000 high-risk women — contrast-enhanced mammography performance at prevalence versus incidence screening rounds. N is 6,911. Multicenter. Direct within-cohort comparison — same women, same centers, different round. You are not comparing across programs with different patient mixes. That design choice is what earns the 0.7 evidence grade, strongest of the week.
JUNO [flat]: So the drop isn't a selection artifact.
CASPAR [measured]: No. And it's substantial enough to matter for resource planning and for the enrollment conversation. The structural explanation is the most parsimonious: a prevalence screen clears existing disease that doesn't replenish at the same rate, so incidence-round yield is lower by design.
JUNO [pressing]: But that's not the only reading.
CASPAR [careful]: Three possibilities the paper doesn't fully disentangle. Appropriate disease clearance — the prevalence round found what was there, incidence round correctly reflects a lower interval development rate. Interval cancer biology — some of what drops out are fast-growing cancers the annual schedule doesn't catch. And a sensitivity limit at incidence rounds that this design doesn't fully expose. I'd rather name all three than let the structural explanation carry more weight than it should.
JUNO [clinical]: Which one should programs actually be worried about?
CASPAR [honest]: Probably some of one and two, with three still open. The structural explanation is real. It doesn't rule out that some of the drop reflects interval cancers CEM may not be catching at follow-up screens. That's the part worth holding rather than resolving prematurely.
JUNO [direct]: So what changes.
CASPAR [specific]: The expectation-setting conversation. Patients and administrators need to hear that first-round yields are not the annual steady state. What does not change is the protocol — no recommendation here to switch modalities at incidence rounds, add supplemental imaging, or adjust intervals. The paper characterizes the gap. It doesn't prescribe a response to it.
JUNO [holding it]: And the question patients will ask when the second-year numbers come in is whether they should be getting something additional at that point. This paper doesn't answer that.
CASPAR [dry]: No. Whether the incidence-screen drop warrants supplemental imaging in year two — and if so, which modality — is the open question. Not answered here.
JUNO [pivot]: Rounds — four more from imaging AI this week.
CASPAR [form-first]: From Radiology AI, 2026: Liu and colleagues, a PRISMA-compliant meta-analysis of 31 studies and over 15,000 patients — anatomy-specific AI performance for large vessel occlusion detection on CT angiography. AI detects ICA and M1 occlusions with high accuracy. Sensitivity drops substantially for distal M2 and M3 segments. Evidence score 0.4 — the 31 included studies have different preprocessing, different outcome definitions, real heterogeneity. Calibration signal, not a deployment verdict.
JUNO [hook]: Ask your vendor specifically about M2 and M3 sensitivity. Not aggregate LVO performance.
CASPAR [next]: From Radiology, 2026: Song and colleagues, a 10-center study with prospective validation — deep learning for bone tumor malignancy risk stratification on plain radiographs. The model integrates clinical and semantic features alongside image data and outperformed both radiologists and Bone-RADS in distinguishing benign from malignant lesions. Evidence score 0.7 — ten centers, prospective test set earns it.
JUNO [caveat]: The disease is rare. The clinical pathway after a high-risk flag still requires musculoskeletal oncology expertise that isn't uniformly available. The centers that most need this tool may not have the referral infrastructure to act on what it finds.
CASPAR [moving]: From Radiology AI, 2026: Zhou and colleagues, a model development study with independent validation — exploiting spatiotemporal mammographic asymmetries for breast cancer risk prediction. The architecture uses both bilateral asymmetry at a single time point and longitudinal asymmetry across serial screens. The combination is the novelty — not either feature alone. Outperformed existing risk models on independent validation. Evidence score 0.4, no prospective clinical trial.
JUNO [distinguishing]: Worth naming — this is the second mammography paper this week and they're asking different questions. Lee et al. is about program-level yield across rounds. This is about individual risk prediction from the images themselves. Different parts of the same workflow.
CASPAR [adding]: And how this sits alongside existing clinical risk calculators isn't answered here. Research tool for now.
CASPAR [final round]: From Lancet Digital Health, 2027: Zhou and colleagues, a foundation model study validated across 31 datasets — MerMED-FM, a self-supervised multimodal imaging model spanning seven modalities and 12 specialties. The claim is robust cross-domain performance without task-specific fine-tuning.
JUNO [flat]: The venue is doing some work that the evidence score doesn't support on its own.
CASPAR [direct]: Evidence score 0.4. Benchmark performance across curated datasets is not deployment performance in a local clinical environment. We've said that about foundation model papers several times this month — the question is the same each time: when does the benchmark become the prerequisite for a prospective trial rather than a substitute for one? That step hasn't happened here.
JUNO [close]: Two things to watch. How much of the CEM incidence-screen drop is appropriate disease clearance versus missed interval cancers — and does the answer change what supplemental imaging looks like in year two. And whether the M2/M3 sensitivity gap in stroke AI is an architecture problem or a resolution ceiling that current CTA can't overcome regardless of model design.
