The clinical question is incremental yield: what does sequencing add after chromosomal microarray comes back negative? CMA catches copy number variants large enough to resolve at the microarray level. Exome and genome sequencing add single-nucleotide variants and small indels that CMA misses entirely. That question has been running on heterogeneous, underpowered estimates for years — different phenotyping protocols, different variant classification schemes, numbers that weren't comparable across studies.
JUNO [clinical]: What makes Dugoff different is the phenotyping infrastructure. Every fetus had detailed imaging across centers, which means the yield figures map onto what you're actually looking at in clinic — not onto some loosely defined referral category.

JUNO [clinical]: So the 30% is trustworthy in a way the prior estimates weren't.
