The result is non-inferiority. Four in-person visits plus two telemedicine contacts versus eight in-person visits. The design earns the claim. The caveat is in the population definition.
JUNO [precise]: Not a permission slip for arbitrary visit reduction. A specific finding about a specific protocol applied to a specific population.
CASPAR [grounded]: The stepped-wedge design is worth a moment. All sites start in the control condition — standard eight-visit schedule — and transition to the reduced-visit intervention at staggered, randomized timepoints. Every cluster eventually receives the intervention. The comparison is within-cluster over time, not between two static arms. That structure handles secular trends and site-level confounding better than a parallel-arm design when you're testing a care-delivery change.
JUNO [precise]: Which puts it above most of the prior evidence on reduced-visit models — before-after studies, largely observational.
CASPAR [grounded]: Correct. Now the population. Low-risk classification was determined at enrollment: singleton, no significant comorbidities, no prior obstetric complications requiring elevated surveillance. Women who developed complications during pregnancy were escalated accordingly. The trial is comparing two static schedules for women who stayed low-risk throughout. It is not testing a dynamic risk-stratification model.
JUNO [precise]: So the load-bearing question for any unit thinking about implementation is whether their initial risk classification is as rigorous as the trial's. If your low-risk category is wider than Stålberg's, you're running a looser version of it.
CASPAR [grounded]: That's the constraint. The safety signal is attached to accurate risk stratification at the outset. Non-inferiority was demonstrated — the reduced-visit arm did not exceed the pre-specified margin for harm across the included sites.
JUNO [precise]: The composite outcome can mask heterogeneity. Whether women closer to the risk boundary have a different experience isn't resolved here.
