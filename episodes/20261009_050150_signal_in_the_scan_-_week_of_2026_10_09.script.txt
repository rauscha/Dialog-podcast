CASPAR [why the miss happens]: The standard ectopic search fails here because it ends at the adnexa. Empty uterus, no adnexal mass — most units stop, or pivot to heterotopic in an IVF patient. The abdominal ectopic doesn't announce itself with a tubal ring. It's not where you're looking.
JUNO [the criteria]: Berg's three-part picture: empty uterus, absent adnexal mass, and an extrauterine gestational sac surrounded by bowel rather than tubal structures. That last criterion is doing the work. A tubal ectopic sits within or adjacent to perisalpingeal tissue. An abdominal ectopic is embedded in peritoneal structures — bowel loops on all sides, no tubal architecture around it.
CASPAR [holding the ceiling]: The sample size is what it is. Multicenter pooling of a rare diagnosis still yields modest case numbers. Sensitivity and specificity against a prospective comparator population haven't been established. These are proposed criteria.
