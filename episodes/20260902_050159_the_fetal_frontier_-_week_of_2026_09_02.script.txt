Every MFM has answered that question — is that the fetus or the operator — and until this week, the honest answer was: we have estimates, but nothing pooled to a standard that makes them comparable across centers.
JUNO [same register]: Prins changes that.
CASPAR [matter-of-fact]: From UOG, 2026: Prins and colleagues, a systematic review and meta-analysis applying TRUST criteria to quantify inter- and intraobserver variability across umbilical artery, middle cerebral artery, and uterine artery Doppler measurements.
JUNO [grounding]: TRUST criteria enforce how variability data gets reported — coefficients of variation, limits of agreement, ICC values, all calculated and presented consistently enough to pool across studies. Without that standard, you're stacking numbers generated under incomparable conditions.
CASPAR [nodding]: Which is why the included pool is smaller than two decades of Doppler reproducibility work would suggest. A lot of that literature doesn't meet the reporting threshold. The filter is what earns the evidence grade, not the volume.
JUNO [pressing]: So what did they actually find?
CASPAR [precise]: Three vessels, two conditions — interobserver and intraobserver. Intraobserver variability is consistently lower than interobserver across all three, formally confirmed rather than assumed. MCA carries higher variability than UA. That matters for CPR interpretation — you're taking a ratio of two measurements, and if the numerator is noisier than the denominator, the composite index inherits that asymmetry.
JUNO [clinically]: Meaning a borderline CPR may be borderline partly because of who measured the MCA.
CASPAR [confirming]: That's the precise statement. For UtA, the variability figures are most relevant to first-trimester screening programs where a single operator often performs both measurements in the same sitting — intraobserver floor there is more favorable, but interobserver spread matters if booking and follow-up are done by different sonographers.
JUNO [pressing the limit]: Give me the number. If a PI shifts ten percent between two studies, is that inside the limits of agreement or outside?
CASPAR [careful]: Vessel-specific, operator-condition-specific — the figures are in the show notes. What I can say is that for interobserver conditions, the limits of agreement are wide enough that a single-point ten percent shift in UA PI sits in genuinely ambiguous territory. Not clearly signal, not clearly noise. Trending across three or more studies is a different calculation than a single-point comparison, and that's the reframe that matters operationally.
JUNO [flat]: So the paper doesn't tell you when to act. It tells you how much uncertainty you were already acting inside.
CASPAR [agreeing]: Correct. And the heterogeneity across included studies means you can't extract a universal threshold from this. Variable operator training, equipment generations, gestational age ranges — the pooled figures are the best available numbers. They are not your center's numbers.
JUNO [holding that]: Which means the argument this paper actually supports is standardized operator assignment in surveillance Doppler programs. Who performed each study is now formally part of the interpretation, not an informal footnote.
CASPAR [dry]: It was always part of the interpretation. Now you can cite a number when you make that argument.
JUNO [small]: That's not nothing.
CASPAR [conceding]: No. And framing it as nothing-yet-changes undersells it. What changes is the evidence quality behind a conversation we were already having informally. The threshold question — what magnitude of serial change should trigger a management decision — is more formally open after this paper. That's a real contribution, even if it doesn't feel like a result.

CASPAR [clean]: From UOG, 2026: Shah and colleagues, a systematic review of long-term outcomes following fetal endoscopic tracheal occlusion for congenital diaphragmatic hernia.
JUNO [finding]: Respiratory morbidity is the most prevalent long-term sequela, and the burden appears to be declining as postnatal care has evolved. Growth faltering is common at one year; most children show catch-up growth thereafter.
CASPAR [immediate caveat]: The heterogeneity across contributing centers is substantial — follow-up protocols differ, outcome definitions differ, the cohorts span different eras of FETO candidacy criteria. Pooled respiratory and growth numbers should not be cited as center-specific figures.
JUNO [clinical hook]: For the family after a FETO referral, this is the first systematic answer to what the first few years look like. Respiratory issues are expected and manageable in most survivors, growth tends to normalize — that's a more grounded counseling frame than survival probability alone.
CASPAR [adding]: The improving respiratory trajectory may partly reflect patient selection shifting as FETO criteria have evolved. Earlier cohort survivors may not be comparable to current candidates. Name that when you cite the directional finding.
JUNO [pivoting]: From UOG, 2026: Sorrenti and colleagues, a cohort study evaluating the prognostic performance of umbilical artery Doppler-based classification in monochorionic twins with selective fetal growth restriction.
CASPAR [finding]: The existing UA Doppler-based classification — Types I, II, III by waveform pattern — does stratify severity in MCDA twins with sFGR. ROC analysis shows limited discriminatory performance for predicting which pregnancies will actually have adverse outcomes.
JUNO [caveat]: Cohort study, specific population — generalizability to centers with different case mix or intervention thresholds is not established.
CASPAR [clinical hook]: Doppler class is one input, not a decision rule. If you're using the classification to time intervention in sFGR twins, the ROC performance means you should be explicit with the family that the class describes a severity gradient, not which pregnancy will deteriorate. This paper names that gap rather than filling it.
JUNO [holding]: This is the most immediately operational finding of the week. Prins changes how you interpret a number. Sorrenti changes what you say about a classification system you're using today.
CASPAR [agreeing, moving]: From UOG, 2026: Bulan and colleagues, a prospective study introducing a modified bilateral multisite umbilical artery Doppler scoring system for early- and late-onset fetal growth restriction.
JUNO [finding]: Sampling the UA at multiple points along the cord bilaterally and generating a composite score correlated with adverse perinatal outcome in both early- and late-onset FGR — the argument being that single-site sampling may underestimate placental resistance heterogeneity.
CASPAR [firm caveat]: Prospective single cohort, novel scoring system, no external validation. Whether the score adds independent prognostic value over standard single-site UA plus MCA plus CPR has not been tested head-to-head. Keep it there.
JUNO [clinical hook]: The conceptual question is worth tracking. If placental resistance varies spatially along the cord, one sampling site may be missing the worst-perfused territory. Multicenter validation is what answers whether that matters clinically.
CASPAR [noting]: Bulan and Prins are in dialogue this week. You can't evaluate whether multisite scoring adds value without knowing how much noise the single-site measurement already carries.
JUNO [last round]: From UOG, 2026: Murillo and colleagues, an observational study examining cardiac remodeling in fetuses exposed to threatened preterm labor who ultimately delivered at term.
CASPAR [measured]: Fetuses who experienced threatened preterm labor without intra-amniotic infection, and subsequently delivered at or near term, showed measurable cardiac remodeling on echocardiography despite normal delivery timing and no infectious mechanism.
JUNO [caveat]: Observational design, no functional outcome data, mechanism not established. Whether the remodeling is hemodynamic, inflammatory, or stress-mediated is unknown. Whether it persists postnatally or carries any cardiovascular consequence is unanswered.
CASPAR [clinical hook]: The population definition is the most important sentence in the paper — no infection, delivered at term. Those two exclusions are what make the finding surprising. The stress of threatened labor itself may be sufficient to leave a cardiac signature. Cohort to follow, not a surveillance mandate.
