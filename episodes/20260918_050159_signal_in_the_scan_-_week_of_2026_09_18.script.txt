CASPAR [grounded]: The lead paper this week asks whether the trained sonographer is load-bearing in first-trimester dating. If you hand an AI model an unstructured sweep — no instruction to find the standard sagittal plane — does it return a gestational age estimate you can use clinically?
JUNO [direct]: Eisenkolb says yes. With caveats.
CASPAR [grounded]: From Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026: Eisenkolb and colleagues, a prospective study of AI applied to non-targeted first-trimester ultrasound sweeps — real-time gestational age estimation without standard biometry. Operators were not instructed to capture standard biometry planes. The model received raw sweep video from 11–14 week examinations and returned a gestational age estimate in real time.
JUNO [precise]: So the clinical question is whether you need the CRL as the dating anchor, or whether a model can pull equivalent information from whatever anatomy the sweep happened to catch.
CASPAR [flat]: That design choice is what earns the evidence grade. Ecological input. Not frames curated for image quality. Whatever the operator acquired.
JUNO [dry]: Which is the failure mode of most of the AI obstetric imaging literature in one sentence.
CASPAR [grounded]: The result: mean absolute error of approximately two days, comparable to expert CRL measurement. Prospective design. That combination — ecologically valid input, prospective acquisition — separates this from prior work in the space.
JUNO [measured]: Two days MAE is within clinical tolerance for first-trimester dating. That's the number that earns it serious attention. I'll say that plainly before we get into what it doesn't tell us.
CASPAR [careful]: Single center. Operator variability in sweep acquisition — speed, angle, patient habitus — is not characterized across environments. The model was trained and tested in one institutional setting. How much of the accuracy is the model and how much is the consistency of that specific acquisition environment is not separable from this paper alone.
JUNO [direct]: And the tail of the error distribution isn't reported with enough granularity to dismiss. Two days on average doesn't tell you about the cases where the estimate is five or seven days off. In first-trimester dating, a seven-day error in a specific case affects screening timing, pregnancy dating, management decisions downstream. That's not a rounding issue.
CASPAR [grounded]: Performance at the edges of the 11–14 week window — not characterized. High BMI, uterine anomalies, fibroids — not characterized. Those aren't generic limitations. They're the specific populations where accurate dating matters most and where image quality is most likely to degrade.
JUNO [flat]: So the cases most likely to produce tail errors are the cases where you most want the model to be right.
CASPAR [confirming]: That's the specific question the external validation trial needs to answer.
JUNO [considered]: The practice implication splits by setting. For low-resource or point-of-care environments where trained sonographers performing standard biometry aren't reliably available — this is a meaningful finding. A model that works on unstructured sweeps could enable accurate dating where the current alternative is no dating, or unreliable dating.
CASPAR [holding]: For high-resource environments where CRL-based dating by trained sonographers is the standard — this paper doesn't justify replacing that workflow. Accuracy is comparable on average. The validation is insufficient to support displacing a well-characterized standard.
JUNO [noting]: The implementation asymmetry is worth naming. The settings that would benefit most from AI-based dating are exactly the settings least likely to run the external validation trials that would establish it. That's a reason to design the validation studies with those populations as the primary target, not an afterthought.
CASPAR [grounded]: Diverse operators, different machines, BMI range, gestational age extremes. That's the trial this paper is asking for.

JUNO [brief]: The clinical question is real. Noninvasive grading before surgery would change planning and counseling. The evidence grade is not there.
CASPAR [flat]: Small N, single center, no external validation. The ovarian radiomics literature has been producing promising pilots that don't survive external validation for several years running. File it, watch for multicenter replication, don't move on it.
JUNO [moving on]: From Radiology, 2026: Li and colleagues, a dual-center evaluation — GPT-5-Thinking assistance for orbital and head-and-neck tumor MRI classification. Generalist radiologist accuracy moved toward subspecialist levels with AI assistance. Dual-center is a genuine step up from most LLM radiology work.
CASPAR [noting]: For sites without neuroradiology coverage interpreting complex orbital and head-and-neck cases, that's a real finding. The improvement needs scrutiny before you generalize — orbital and head-and-neck tumors are a narrow case mix, and the liability question when an LLM-assisted read misses something a subspecialist would have caught is not answered here.
JUNO [continuing]: From Radiology, 2026: Fu and colleagues, a prospective cohort — photon-counting CT versus MRI, mammography, and PET/CT for pretreatment breast cancer staging. Staging performance came out comparable to MRI, superior to mammography.
CASPAR [measured]: MRI carries access, cost, and time constraints, so a modality that matches its performance with faster acquisition is worth tracking. But MRI remains the reference standard, photon-counting CT isn't widely deployed, and the question of whether it holds across the presentations that make MRI indispensable — not just the cases where both modalities perform well — isn't answered. Feasibility established. Displacement not supported.
JUNO [grounded]: From Radiology, 2026: Hathaway and colleagues, longitudinal data from the MESA cohort — opportunistic chest CT paraspinal muscle biomarkers predicting vertebral bone loss. MESA is the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis, large multicenter longitudinal cohort. Low paraspinal muscle attenuation — a CT measure of muscle quality, lower values reflecting greater fat infiltration — independently predicted subsequent vertebral bone mineral density loss and fracture.
CASPAR [grounded]: A soft-tissue measurement on a scan obtained for another indication carrying skeletal prognostic information that isn't currently extracted in routine CT reporting. Large, multicenter, longitudinal. Strongest evidence grade of the rounds.
JUNO [pressing]: And the open question is whether it gets integrated into automated CT reporting pipelines or joins the list of validated signals that nobody acts on. External validation of the Eisenkolb model — diverse operators, different machines, high-BMI populations, the edge cases the single-center design can't characterize.
