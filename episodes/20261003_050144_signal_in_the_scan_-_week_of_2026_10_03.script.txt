CASPAR [grounded]: From JAMIA, 2026: Liu and colleagues, a co-learning NLP framework applied across 38 million PubMed abstracts — mapping pharmacotherapy knowledge gaps in pregnant, breastfeeding, and pediatric populations. Five populations: pregnant, breastfeeding, neonatal, pediatric, adolescent. Co-learning means the maternal and pediatric corpora train jointly — patterns of absence in one population inform what the model looks for in the other. Output is ranked drug-indication pairs by evidence density. Not effect estimates. A density map of the literature.
JUNO [clinical]: So concretely — what does a flagged gap look like?
CASPAR [specific]: A drug class appearing with minimal indexed evidence across a gestational trimester or a pediatric age band. The framework quantifies how thin the coverage is, not what the drug does. No dosing recommendations come out of this. You get a ranked list of where the literature is essentially silent.
JUNO [measured]: Which is still a real contribution. The prior state was someone on a grant committee asserting an area was underserved. This replaces that with a systematic output from a defined process.
CASPAR [holding]: It does. And then the ceiling lands. The framework mines abstracts only — not full text.
