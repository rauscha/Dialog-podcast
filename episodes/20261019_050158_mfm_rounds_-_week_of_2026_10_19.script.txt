JUNO [direct]: From Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026: Liu and colleagues, a multicenter double-blind RCT — low-intensity pulsed ultrasound to facilitate uterine involution.
CASPAR [grounded]: LIPUS — non-thermal, non-pharmacologic pulsed acoustic energy applied externally to the postpartum uterus. Not imaging. The transducer is delivering mechanical stimulus to the myometrium, not producing a picture.
JUNO [pressing]: And involution as the primary endpoint — why does that qualify as a hard endpoint rather than an intermediate marker?
CASPAR [clear]: Delayed involution produces the complications you're trying to prevent. Secondary hemorrhage, endometritis, readmission. You're not measuring it as a proxy for something else — subinvolution is the problem. Liu reports both: accelerated involution on serial measurement and reduced postpartum complications in the LIPUS group versus sham. Multicenter enrollment, which matters for external validity in a way a single-site study can't claim.
JUNO [following]: Walk me through why the sham control is the load-bearing design choice here.
CASPAR [deliberate]: Blinding a physical intervention is technically difficult. If patients know they're getting active treatment, you've introduced attention effects, positioning differences, clinician behavior differences. A sham the patient cannot distinguish from active treatment is what separates this from an open-label comparison. Without it, you can't isolate how much of the effect is LIPUS and how much is the act of applying a transducer to the abdomen for a defined period. That design choice is what earns the level-one designation.
JUNO [conceding]: So the design earns the grade. What doesn't it answer?
CASPAR [measured]: Two things. Mechanism first — how LIPUS accelerates involution at the tissue level is incompletely characterized. Proposed pathways involve myometrial contractility and local inflammatory modulation, but the paper doesn't establish the mechanism cleanly.
JUNO [probing]: Does that matter for adoption? If the RCT shows the effect—
CASPAR [interrupting, dry]: For a non-pharmacologic intervention with a favorable safety profile, the RCT result carries more weight than the mechanism. But an incomplete mechanism limits your ability to predict which subpopulations respond, and it's a harder argument when someone on the protocol committee asks why it works.
JUNO [accepting that]: And the second thing?
CASPAR [specific]: Population generalizability. We don't yet know the delivery mode distribution in the trial, the baseline hemorrhage risk profile of the enrolled patients, or how this performs in settings with higher obstetric acuity. Whether the complication reduction holds in a unit with a different case mix is untested. This result warrants pulling the paper. It does not warrant ordering equipment or revising a postpartum order set. Replication in diverse settings is the next required step.
