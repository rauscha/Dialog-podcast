CASPAR [anchor]: From AJOG, 2026: Chatzakis and colleagues, a prospective cohort of nearly 12,000 women — what uterine artery Doppler actually reflects in late pregnancy preeclampsia.
JUNO [framing]: The question is specific. Not whether late UtA PI predicts — we already knew it predicts. What it's actually measuring when it does.
CASPAR [design]: The design move that earns the confidence here is concurrent measurement. Three distinct marker classes, same gestational window, same women. Angiogenic markers — sFlt-1/PlGF ratio. Maternal hemodynamics — cardiac output, peripheral resistance. Vascular stiffness indices. Not sequential, not in separate cohorts. Simultaneously, prospectively, nearly 12,000 women.
JUNO [precise]: You can't ask what the waveform reflects if your measurements of the candidate pathways come from different populations at different times. The simultaneity is the whole argument.
CASPAR [finding]: The result: elevated late UtA PI correlates across all three streams in women who develop preeclampsia. Angiogenic imbalance. Altered hemodynamics. Increased vascular stiffness. All three, in the same women, at the same window.
JUNO [counterintuitive]: The field has been treating this primarily as a placental signal — elevated downstream resistance in the uteroplacental circulation. What Chatzakis shows is that it reflects systemic maternal vascular adaptation and endothelial dysfunction just as much. It's not one pathway. Multiple pathways converging on the same waveform.
CASPAR [precision]: The word is correlates. Observational design, no intervention arm, no causal attribution established. The triangulation is real. Directionality is not proven by this paper.
JUNO [implication]: Which actually explains why the Doppler worked as a screening signal even when the mechanistic account was incomplete. It was integrating information no single biomarker captures alone.
CASPAR [caveat]: And that's also why the honest answer on practice change is: not yet. The paper strengthens the rationale for late Doppler in risk stratification. It does not define a UtA PI threshold at 35–36 weeks that should trigger altered management. That threshold work hasn't been done.
JUNO [pushes]: Though — it moves the argument for doing that work. Before this, you were screening on a correlation without a clean account of what you were measuring. Now you have the mechanistic foundation. Angiogenic, hemodynamic, vascular stiffness. That's a different starting point for designing the threshold study.
CASPAR [revises]: Fair. The foundation is now there in a way it wasn't before. The open question is what cutoff at 35–36 weeks, and what you do when you cross it. Neither is in this paper, but the case for doing the work is stronger.
