CASPAR [carrying]: From Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026: Watananirun and colleagues, a multicenter longitudinal cohort — lung growth response to FETO by CDH laterality and occlusion duration.
JUNO [pressing]: Why did this specifically need a multicenter longitudinal design?
CASPAR [accepting]: Single-center cross-sectional data on right-sided FETO response existed before this. The problem is that a cross-sectional snapshot can't separate baseline lung size from treatment trajectory. Right-sided cases often start with more compressed lungs — liver herniation patterns, mediastinal shift — so a smaller absolute o/e-LHR at any single timepoint tells you almost nothing about whether FETO is actually working. You need serial measurements across the same patients over time to see the growth curve. And you need enough centers that one program's patient selection isn't driving the whole result.
JUNO [nodding]: So the design earns its evidence grade because it can actually answer the trajectory question.
CASPAR [confirming]: Serial o/e-LHR tracked longitudinally, multicenter, occlusion duration recorded as a continuous variable. That combination is what makes this the headline.
JUNO [direct]: What do the curves show?
CASPAR [measured]: Right-sided CDH shows a measurably attenuated lung growth trajectory compared to left-sided cases undergoing the same intervention. The o/e-LHR gains are smaller on the right. And across both lateralities, longer tracheal occlusion duration correlates with greater o/e-LHR gain.
JUNO [picking up]: So longer occlusion is a lever.
CASPAR [careful]: It's a correlate — and I want to hold that. Longer duration may also be selecting for cases where operators waited because early response was poor. Cases that weren't responding got more time, and still ended up with better gains than they'd have had otherwise. The association is real. The causal claim isn't clean from this design.
JUNO [accepting, then pressing]: Even so — if you're sitting across from a right-sided family before consent, you're no longer extrapolating from left-sided data. You're telling them gains are likely to be smaller, that longer occlusion may be needed to get there, and that the balloon removal timing and the preterm delivery risk discussion look different for them than for a left-sided case.
CASPAR [granting it]: That's supportable from what's here.
CASPAR [shifting]: The anatomic question is the one I want to name before this gets pushed too far. Right-sided CDH is rarer and more heterogeneous. The attenuated response may just reflect cases starting from a more compressed baseline that need more time — a duration problem, not a biology problem. If it's anatomy, longer occlusion is the answer and the underlying FETO mechanism is the same. If it's a genuine biologic difference in efficacy for right-sided cases, some of them may not respond adequately regardless of how long you wait. This paper demonstrates attenuation. It does not resolve which of those two things is happening.
JUNO [following]: For the family in front of you, the mechanism may matter less than the fact that conservative expectations are now backed by multicenter data. You're not guessing.

CASPAR [carrying]: From Prenatal Diagnosis, 2026: Xu and colleagues, a validation study of trio-exome Mendelian inheritance error analysis — detecting UPD and resolving discordant NIPT findings. The pipeline uses Mendelian inheritance errors — positions where the child's genotype is incompatible with one parent's — to flag uniparental disomy. The technically important piece is heterodisomy. Standard AOH methods miss heterodisomies by design; there's no loss of heterozygosity to detect. MIE analysis catches them through the inheritance pattern. In their validation cohort, the pipeline resolved more than half of discordant NIPT cases and unexplained FGR cases where standard workup had stalled.
JUNO [naming the use case]: NIPT flags a mosaic pattern that doesn't fit the picture, or FGR without an obvious etiology — that's when you escalate to trio exome rather than stopping at microarray. A UPD diagnosis changes recurrence counseling in a way microarray alone won't give you.
CASPAR [adding the limit]: Trio availability isn't universal, and the cohort is enriched for cases where something already didn't add up. Performance in unselected populations isn't characterized here.
