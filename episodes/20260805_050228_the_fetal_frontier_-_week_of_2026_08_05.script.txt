CASPAR [formal citation]: From Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026: Wu and colleagues, a development and external validation cohort study — prognostic ultrasound markers to predict pump-twin survival in twin reversed arterial perfusion sequence.
JUNO [pressing]: Design structure before numbers.
CASPAR [methodical]: Markers developed in one cohort, tested in a separate external population. Validation cases were not used to build the model — that's the minimum bar, and it's the bar most TRAP literature hasn't cleared. The inputs include acardiac-to-pump twin ratio, Doppler indices, and gestational age at presentation. Output is pump-twin survival.
JUNO [focused]: Direction of the effect?
CASPAR [precise]: Higher acardiac-to-pump ratio and abnormal Doppler indices at presentation associate with worse pump-twin outcomes. The validated cutoffs stratify who needs intervention now versus who can be observed.
JUNO [clinically]: So the protocol-committee conversation shifts. Not "what does your center usually do at this ratio" — "what does the validated model say the risk is at this ratio."
CASPAR [measured]: Those are genuinely different conversations. I'd rate evidence confidence medium — the design earns the grade, but I want to name the constraint before we go further.
JUNO [anticipating]: The N.
CASPAR [confirming]: TRAP is rare. Even a validation cohort is modest in absolute numbers. That's not a criticism of the study — it's a structural feature of the condition. A model validated in a small external cohort may perform differently in centers with different referral patterns, different gestational-age distributions, different intervention protocols. The markers earn credibility from the design step. Not from large N.

The argument you bring to the protocol committee is stronger. The protocol committee still has to make the decision.
JUNO [holding both]: And for centers that have been operating on one experienced clinician's gestalt, that reference point matters. It's portable in a way gestalt isn't.

JUNO [formal]: From Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026: Sirico and colleagues, a systematic review and meta-analysis — left and right myocardial performance indices in growth-restricted fetuses.
CASPAR [consolidating]: Pooled data confirm bilateral MPI elevation and diastolic dysfunction in FGR, with early-onset showing more pronounced cardiac changes than late-onset. Strongest design in the rounds this week.

JUNO [distinction first]: The distinction that has to land before the finding makes sense: monozygosity is embryological — identical twins from a single zygote. Monochorionicity is hemodynamic — shared placentation with vascular connections. They overlap, but a monozygotic pair can be dichorionic.
