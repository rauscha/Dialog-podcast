CASPAR [carrying]: From JAMIA, 2026: Liu and colleagues, a co-learning NLP framework applied to 38 million PubMed abstracts — mapping maternal and pediatric pharmacotherapy research gaps. The architecture pairs the two domains so that evidence patterns in one inform gap detection in the other. That cross-domain structure is what separates this from a large-corpus keyword search — gaps appearing in both maternal and pediatric data carry more weight in the ranking than gaps appearing in only one. Output is a knowledge graph of drug-population pairs ranked by clinical relevance weighting, organized by drug class and gestational window. High-volume prescribing categories with absent evidence surface above low-volume edge cases.
JUNO [pressing]: What does the ranking actually surface? Which classes are worst?
CASPAR [measured]: The paper identifies persistent deficits concentrated in specific drug classes and trimester windows — the framework makes those visible as ranked, reproducible outputs rather than expert-asserted gaps. For the specific leading classes, pull the full paper. The structural point is real: for anyone writing a guideline section or designing a trial, you now have a citable, systematic output rather than expert consensus. That changes the quality of the argument to a research committee.
JUNO [noting]: And the ceiling?
CASPAR [precise]: Abstract-only mining. Full-text findings, unpublished trial registries, clinical outcome data — none of that is in scope.
