CASPAR [grounded]: Every clinician managing a pregnant patient with a chronic condition has made a pharmacotherapy call without a trial to cite. That's not an oversight — it's the structural condition of the field. What nobody has done until now is enumerate those gaps systematically, at scale, with something more rigorous than expert intuition.
JUNO [direct]: From JAMIA, 2026: Liu and colleagues, a co-learning NLP framework applied to 38 million PubMed abstracts — mapping pharmacotherapy evidence gaps in pregnant, breastfeeding, and pediatric populations.
CASPAR [methodical]: The design choice that makes the scale possible is a co-learning architecture — two NLP modules trained jointly. One does named entity recognition: drugs, populations, study types. The other does relation extraction: which drugs appear in studies of which populations, and in what role. Training them together improves coherence between what the system labels and what it infers. The output is a knowledge graph — drug-population pairs with literature density scores. Sparse coverage flags a gap. The result is a prioritized list by drug class and clinical domain.
JUNO [pressing]: So it's counting how often a drug appears alongside a population in the abstract literature, then ranking by how thin that coverage is.
CASPAR [precise]: With a clinical relevance weighting on top. The prioritization tries to flag gaps where prescribing frequency is high and evidence is low — not just where publication counts are low. That's where it attempts to do more than count abstracts.
JUNO [noting the ceiling]: Which is also where the evidence grade matters. Retrospective text mining. Validated against a curated subset, not a prospective clinical audit.
CASPAR [confirming]: Informatics-grade, yes. The system's accuracy depends on how well the NLP captures study-design nuance — a trial that enrolled pregnant patients incidentally reads differently than one where they were the primary population. Co-learning helps, but that distinction is genuinely hard to resolve from abstract text alone.
JUNO [on the output]: What did the gap map actually show?
CASPAR [delivering]: Substantial literature sparsity across a wide range of drug classes in pregnancy and lactation. Pediatric populations showed the most heterogeneous coverage — some drug classes reasonably well-studied in children, many not. The prioritization scores rank gaps by clinical relevance weighting, which is the operationally useful piece. For a funder or a guideline committee, this is a concrete, reproducible starting point rather than an expert panel's best guess about where the holes are.
JUNO [testing]: And the central limitation is that a gap in the published literature doesn't mean filling it would change outcomes or prescribing behavior.
CASPAR [holding the line]: Right. Some gaps exist because the drug is rarely used in these populations. Others exist because the population was excluded from trials despite high clinical need. The framework cannot reliably distinguish those two scenarios from abstract text. A drug that appears rarely in the maternal literature because it's rarely prescribed is a different problem from one that appears rarely because every trial excluded pregnant women.
JUNO [practical]: So the audience is funders and trialists. Not the bedside.
CASPAR [flat]: Nothing changes in prescribing today. What changes is the quality of the argument you can make to a research committee about where to direct the next trial. You're replacing expert intuition with a ranked, reproducible output from a systematic process. That's a real advance for research prioritization. It is not a clinical tool.
JUNO [quiet]: And whether that advance translates into funded trials is a question this paper can't answer.
CASPAR [same economy]: The map is the contribution. What happens to it next is not in this paper.

CASPAR [clean]: From Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, 2026: Cantone and colleagues, a reader study of DeepVEST — deep-learning vessel segmentation and erasure in breast MRI maximum intensity projections. Vessels obscured lesion margins in roughly 60 percent of breast MRI MIPs in this cohort. After DeepVEST removes them, radiologists reported improved margin visibility across the reader cohort.
JUNO [on the caveat]: Improved margin visibility in a reader study is not improved diagnostic accuracy. Perception study, not an outcome trial. The link to biopsy yield or reduced recall rates hasn't been demonstrated. Watch for downstream work connecting this to biopsy concordance before it earns a place in the protocol.
CASPAR [next]: From Lancet Digital Health, 2026: Shiri and colleagues, a multicenter development and validation study — multimodal AI for long-term mortality prediction after TAVI. A model fusing clinical data, ECG, echocardiography, CT, and catheterization findings outperformed existing risk scores for long-term post-procedural mortality across two international centers.
JUNO [on the stakes]: The argument is that the data required for better risk stratification is already being collected before TAVI — it just isn't being integrated at the time of decision. Two external validation sites is a thin generalizability argument, and the model's advantage only holds if all five input modalities are reliably available pre-procedure, which isn't universal in lower-volume centers.
CASPAR [brief]: Real-time workflow integration is untested. Multicenter validation is meaningful; deployment readiness is not established.
JUNO [moving]: From Lancet Digital Health, 2026: Yew and colleagues, a retrospective comparative study across multiple centers — benchmarking the RETFound retinal foundation model against conventional deep learning. RETFound matched but rarely outperformed conventional architectures for retinal and systemic disease detection — including in low-label scenarios where foundation models are supposed to show their advantage.
CASPAR [on the significance]: The assumption driving foundation model investment in medical imaging is that pre-training on large, domain-specific datasets reduces the labeled-data requirement for downstream tasks. Yew challenges that assumption directly, in the domain where RETFound was purpose-built. That's an uncomfortable place for the result to land.
JUNO [calibrating]: One domain, one model, one benchmark design. Calibration, not a verdict on foundation models broadly. But benchmark before assuming a foundation model will save labeling effort — the superiority isn't automatic, and this paper says so in the domain that should have been the easy case.
CASPAR [last round]: From Radiology, 2026: Cai and colleagues, a single-center retrospective study — MRI-guided versus standard OSEM reconstruction for amyloid brain PET. MRI-guided reconstruction improved inter-reader agreement and Centiloid separation compared to standard OSEM in this cohort.
JUNO [on the stakes]: Inter-reader variability at the Centiloid threshold used to guide anti-amyloid therapy decisions is a real clinical problem. If MRI-guided reconstruction compresses that variability, the downstream effect is more consistent patient management.
CASPAR [on the limit]: Single-center, retrospective. Clinical impact on management is not demonstrated. Worth tracking as hybrid PET-MRI workflows expand; it doesn't travel far today.
