The framework mines the published literature for evidence density by drug class and population, and outputs a knowledge graph ranking drug-population pairs by how underrepresented they are relative to clinical relevance weighting.

CASPAR [citation]: From Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, 2026: Cantone and colleagues, a reader study of DeepVEST — a deep learning vessel-erasure tool for breast MRI. The problem: on maximum intensity projection images, enhancing vessels can sit directly over lesions. DeepVEST segments and digitally removes those vessels before interpretation. In this reader study, lesion assessment improved when the vascular structures were erased.
JUNO [precise]: Reader study, not an outcomes trial. Better detection in a controlled reading exercise hasn't been linked to biopsy yield or recall rates yet.
CASPAR [agreeing]: For centers interpreting breast MRI in high-risk patients, this is a workflow tool worth tracking toward clearance. Not a protocol change. Watch for the outcomes data.
CASPAR [citation]: From Lancet Digital Health, 2026: Yew and colleagues, a multicenter retrospective comparison of RETFound, a retina-specific foundation model, against standard supervised deep learning for ocular and systemic disease detection. RETFound was purpose-built for retinal imaging — large ophthalmic pretraining dataset, tested in its own domain. The investment thesis rests on pretraining at scale reducing labeled-data requirements and improving downstream performance. Yew tests that directly. RETFound did not consistently outperform standard supervised models across tasks.
JUNO [calibrating]: One domain, one model, one benchmark design. Calibration, not a verdict on foundation models broadly.
CASPAR [holding]: Agreed — but this is the domain where the argument for outperformance was strongest. If it doesn't hold there, the assumption that domain-specific pretraining is an automatic upgrade needs more scrutiny than it's been getting. Benchmark before you assume the foundation model will save your labeling effort.
CASPAR [citation]: From Radiology, 2026: Cai and colleagues, a single-center retrospective study of MRI-guided versus OSEM reconstruction for amyloid brain PET. MRI-guided reconstruction uses structural MRI data to sharpen the PET signal. The finding: improved inter-reader agreement and better quantitative separation between amyloid-positive and amyloid-negative scans. For neuroradiology colleagues, the inter-reader agreement number is the operationally relevant piece — a borderline scan two readers call differently is a patient who gets inconsistent counseling.
JUNO [brief]: Single-center, retrospective. Thin generalizability until multicenter data arrive.
CASPAR [confirming]: The technical advance is real. Deployment readiness is downstream.
CASPAR [citation]: From Radiology, 2026: Han and colleagues, a prospective five-year follow-up using quantitative CT and hyperpolarized xenon-129 ventilation MRI in COVID-19 pneumonia survivors. Xenon-129 MRI maps regional ventilation — a functional dimension quantitative CT alone can't access. Persistent pulmonary abnormalities at five years, including in patients whose initial illness was nonsevere, compared to matched controls. Longer follow-up and broader severity range than most prior data.
