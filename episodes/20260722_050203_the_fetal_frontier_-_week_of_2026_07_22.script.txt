CASPAR [measured]: Thoracoamniotic shunting in macrocystic CPAM reduced lesion size. It also showed lower survival and higher prematurity in the intervention arm. Before either of those numbers does any work, the confounding has to land.
JUNO [direct]: The survival finding is real in the data. The causal attribution is not. Holding both simultaneously is the work this week.
CASPAR [grounding]: From Prenatal Diagnosis, 2026: Carmant and colleagues, a multicenter cohort comparing thoracoamniotic shunting versus expectant management in macrocystic CPAM. Outcomes across lesion size, survival, and prematurity, stratified by CVR greater than two and hydrops status.
JUNO [pressing]: What earns the evidence grade?
CASPAR [plain]: The multicenter structure — this isn't one center's case series. The ceiling is the absence of randomization. Stratifying by CVR and hydrops is the methodologically important move; it's the only thing that gets you partway toward comparing like with like.
JUNO [flat]: Partway.
CASPAR [dry]: Partway. The shunted and expectant groups are not symmetric at baseline, and no stratification fully corrects for that.
CASPAR [continuing]: What the data showed: shunting reduced lesion size — that held. Survival was lower in the shunted arm. Prematurity was higher. CVR greater than two and hydrops at presentation were the dominant mortality predictors across both arms.
JUNO [immediate]: And the shunted fetuses were the ones with those features.
CASPAR [confirming]: Almost certainly. Intervention was selected for deteriorating cases — higher CVR, developing hydrops. That selection is invisible in a simple arm-to-arm survival comparison. Sicker fetuses got the procedure. Sicker fetuses died more. You cannot read that as the procedure causing the worse outcome.
JUNO [holding]: So what can you actually read it as?
CASPAR [careful]: That shunting reduced lesion size and didn't overcome the prognostic weight of CVR greater than two and hydrops. That's a more honest sentence than "shunting is harmful." The intervention didn't rescue the mortality risk those features carry.
JUNO [pressing]: What changes in practice?
CASPAR [deliberate]: Stable macrocystic CPAM — no hydrops, CVR below two — expectant management is defensible and the data argue for it as the default. Shunting reserved for fetuses showing clear deterioration crossing those thresholds. The indication is narrowed, not the procedure discredited.
JUNO [noting the gap]: For centers currently shunting on lesion size alone, that's a real recalibration.
CASPAR [blunt]: It is. And the only thing that resolves this cleanly is a randomized trial. Recruitment will be hard — families and centers self-select strongly in this space. The field may be working with confounded observational data for a long time. Better to name that than pretend it's temporary.
CASPAR [pivoting]: Rounds — four other things this week.
CASPAR [formal]: From Prenatal Diagnosis, 2026: Cruz-Martínez and colleagues, a prospective study on fetal position effects on O/E-LHR measurement in left-sided CDH. O/E-LHR is systematically overestimated by approximately eleven percent when the lung is probe-proximal versus probe-distal.
JUNO [sharp]: Eleven percent moves fetuses across severity categories.
CASPAR [confirming]: It does. A fetus classified as severe enough to discuss FETO at one scan position may not meet threshold at another. Evidence score 0.4 — prospective but limited — and the direction of the error is geometrically consistent enough to act on now. Standardize fetal position at acquisition, or at minimum document it when reporting O/E-LHR.
CASPAR [continuing]: From Prenatal Diagnosis, 2026: D'Amico and colleagues, a systematic review and meta-analysis of cardiac function parameters in fetal growth restriction across fifteen studies. Consistent findings: altered ventricular geometry, impaired diastolic function, reduced global longitudinal strain. Evidence score 1.0 — highest-confidence paper in the rounds.
JUNO [noting]: Fifteen studies converging on the same parameters is a different claim than any one center's observation.
CASPAR [holding the caveat]: Consistent signal, yes. Actionable decision thresholds, no. At what degree of diastolic dysfunction do you move on delivery timing? That question isn't answered here. The case for incorporating functional echocardiography into FGR surveillance is substantially stronger. The cutoffs are still missing.
CASPAR [continuing]: From Prenatal Diagnosis, 2026: Birene and colleagues, a twelve-year single-center cohort tracking pregnancy outcomes after increased first-trimester nuchal translucency of three-point-five millimeters or greater. Among pregnancies continuing after normal genetic and structural workup, eighty-two percent resulted in healthy live births.
JUNO [immediate]: That's a usable number for families who've completed the workup and are deciding whether to continue.
CASPAR [adding the condition]: With that specificity explicit. The eighty-two percent is the post-normal-workup group — it doesn't apply before the workup is done, and single-center design limits how far it travels. There's a second finding worth naming: termination without diagnostic testing rising with NT thickness. Families opting out of the pathway entirely before any genetic or structural information is in hand. That's a different counseling problem than the family who completes the workup and then decides.
CASPAR [continuing]: From Prenatal Diagnosis, 2026: Bradford and colleagues, a physiology-based analysis linking fetal cerebrovascular resistance to two-year neurodevelopment in congenital heart disease. In CHD fetuses with systemic outflow obstruction, lower MCA-PI — conventionally read as brain-sparing — was paradoxically associated with better two-year neurodevelopmental outcomes.
JUNO [registering]: That's the wrong direction by standard interpretation.
CASPAR [explaining]: Standard MCA-PI interpretation runs on placental insufficiency physiology. In systemic outflow obstruction, the cerebral perfusion dynamics are different. The brain-sparing framework doesn't port cleanly into CHD, and if it's being applied reflexively to CHD surveillance it may be pointing the wrong way. Evidence score 0.4, single-center, mechanism not established — this needs replication across lesion subtypes before it touches any protocol. It's a signal that CHD-specific Doppler interpretation is overdue, not a reason to change what you're doing at next week's scan.
JUNO [accepting that]: Two open questions, then.
CASPAR [naming them]: Whether a randomized CPAM shunting trial is feasible, or whether the field is permanently working with confounded observational data. And whether the Bradford MCA-PI inversion replicates across CHD lesion subtypes and what the mechanism actually is. Neither is answered this week. DOIs for all five papers are in the show notes.
