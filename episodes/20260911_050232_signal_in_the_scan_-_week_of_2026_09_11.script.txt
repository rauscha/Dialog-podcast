CASPAR [flat, setting the table]: Signal in the Scan, week of September eleventh. The lead paper this week closes a specific gap — not in what AI can detect, but in whether AI detection improves what happens during a live scan.
JUNO [picking up]: Retrospective validation tells you the model performs on curated data. It doesn't tell you whether it changes what a sonographer catches in real time. That's the question the Lei trial actually answers.
CASPAR [into the headline]: From *Lancet Digital Health*, 2026: Lei and colleagues, a multicenter self-crossover randomized controlled trial in China — AI-assisted detection of fetal intracranial malformations during real-time prenatal ultrasound.
JUNO [clinical framing]: The target is the routine anatomy scan. Ventriculomegaly, agenesis of the corpus callosum, posterior fossa anomalies — these aren't misses at referral centers. They're misses when an operator has limited dwell time and the prior probability is low. That's the right problem to design around.
CASPAR [on the design]: The crossover structure is what earns the evidence grade. Same sonographers, both arms — AI active in one, not in the other. Operator is their own control. The standard objection to these trials is that better detection in the AI arm might just reflect better operators or a more favorable case mix. This design forecloses that. Multiple centers across China — not one cooperative site with one cooperative vendor.
JUNO [brief]: So what did it show?
CASPAR [on the numbers]: Detection rates of fetal intracranial malformations improved significantly when AI was running. The research package doesn't confirm the absolute baseline miss rate numerically — I'm not going to cite a percentage I can't verify — but the direction and statistical significance are established. In a prospective, controlled setting, the AI effect is real and measurable.
JUNO [pressing on practice]: For a center already in a procurement conversation —
CASPAR [cutting in]: It moves the conversation. You now have prospective, controlled evidence that the effect exists in a live workflow. That's different from validation data. What it doesn't do is let you import the result to your platform, your operators, or your population.
JUNO [naming it]: China only.
CASPAR [holding the line]: One AI platform, sonographers trained in Chinese imaging pipelines, a population with its own baseline prevalence of these malformations. Platform specificity in AI imaging is real — a model validated on one equipment ecosystem doesn't automatically transfer. And baseline detection rates matter here, because how much room there is for AI to improve depends on where your operators start. None of that is characterized outside this dataset.
JUNO [not dismissing it]: Which makes the replication question specific, not fatal. The crossover design is the template. Someone needs to run it on different platforms with different operator baselines.
CASPAR [adding the harder thread]: And the other open question isn't a design problem at all. When AI flags something the operator didn't see — how do you train appropriate reliance? Not reflexive override, not reflexive deference. That's a human factors problem. No RCT has touched it. It's also the question that determines whether implementation actually works outside a controlled trial.
JUNO [dry]: It's what sits underneath every procurement conversation and doesn't get asked.
CASPAR [closing the headline]: Both threads named. Neither answered this week.
JUNO [pivot]: Rounds — four other things this week.
CASPAR [Round 1, brisk]: From *Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology*, 2026: Sorrenti and colleagues, a 24-year cohort study — prognostic performance of umbilical artery Doppler classification in monochorionic pregnancies with selective fetal growth restriction. The Types I through III classification stratified severity and predicted adverse outcomes across that cohort. Twenty-four years is a real denominator for a tool already embedded in surveillance protocols.
JUNO [the caveat, flat]: Most cases were Type I.
CASPAR [holding it]: The mildest phenotype. Which means the prognostic power in Types II and III — where counseling is hardest — is the part of this dataset that's least powered. The protocol reinforcement is real. The confidence at the severe end is thinner than the headline suggests.
JUNO [Round 2]: From *Radiology*, 2026: Lin and colleagues, a multicenter external validation across four centers — AI detection of radiograph-negative femoral neck fractures using a model called OccuNet. Matched radiologist accuracy, exceeded emergency physicians across all four external sites. That's the right comparison — the ED physician reading the initial film is where the miss-and-harm scenario actually lives.
CASPAR [the gap]: The validation is solid. What the paper doesn't define is the downstream pathway. AI-positive, X-ray-negative — does that patient go straight to CT? MRI? A validation study doesn't answer that. The flag is a prompt. The protocol is something else.
JUNO [Round 3, clipped]: From *Radiology*, 2026: Deipolyi and colleagues, a single-center RCT of 264 procedures — ketamine plus midazolam versus fentanyl plus midazolam for sedation during interventional radiology. Ketamine arm reduced intraprocedural pain. Clean result, narrow population. File it if you're involved in IR-guided procedures or shared sedation protocols.
CASPAR [Round 4]: From *Radiology*, 2026: Paramasamy and colleagues, a large retrospective real-world study at a single tertiary center — commercial AI impact on radiologist reading time for pulmonary nodule evaluation at chest CT. Reading time dropped modestly with AI active. One center, retrospective — if you're in a nodule AI procurement conversation, this is a data point. It doesn't close the argument.
JUNO [close]: Two things still open from Lei. Whether the platform generalizes — different equipment, different operator pipelines, different populations. And the trust-calibration problem: how do you train a sonographer to know when to push back on the flag? Not a model performance question. A training design question. Neither answered.
