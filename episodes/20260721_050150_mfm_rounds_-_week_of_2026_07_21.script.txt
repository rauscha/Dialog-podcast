CASPAR [direct]: The ALPS trial established that antenatal betamethasone reduces respiratory morbidity in late preterm infants. It also established higher rates of neonatal hypoglycemia. What it didn't tell us was whether that hypoglycemia — glucose below 40 mg/dL, often managed as transient in the NICU — leaves anything behind at school age. That question now has an answer.
JUNO [flat]: And the answer changes what you say before you give the steroids.
CASPAR [measured]: From Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2026: Gyamfi-Bannerman and colleagues, the long-term follow-up of the ALPS trial cohort — neurodevelopmental outcomes at age six or older in late preterm infants who experienced neonatal hypoglycemia.
JUNO [pressing]: Design first.
CASPAR [precise]: Multicenter RCT, original randomization to betamethasone versus placebo, enrollment closed 2015. The follow-up is prospective neurodevelopmental assessment with standardized outcome ascertainment at age six or older. The structural weight comes from that original randomization. But the comparison here is between infants within the cohort who developed hypoglycemia and those who didn't — that exposure was not itself randomized. Observational comparison inside a trial. Not the same thing as a randomized comparison of hypoglycemia exposure.
JUNO [direct]: How common was glucose below 40 in this population post-steroids?
CASPAR [dry]: Not rare. Late preterm infants already carry elevated baseline hypoglycemia risk. Steroid exposure amplifies it. The original ALPS publication documented higher rates in the betamethasone arm — that's been in the literature for years. This follow-up asks what those events cost developmentally.
JUNO [flat]: And they cost something.
CASPAR [measured]: Yes. Hypoglycemia below 40 mg/dL in the late preterm period was associated with adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes at school age. The association is clinically meaningful, not just statistically detectable.
JUNO [direct]: Late preterm infants are already at baseline neurodevelopmental risk relative to term. You're adding a metabolic exposure on top of that.
CASPAR [confirming]: Right. The steroid-exposed subset with documented hypoglycemia is the group carrying additional risk above that baseline. And most of us have not been naming that explicitly in the counseling room.
JUNO [pressing]: What keeps you from claiming causation?
CASPAR [careful]: The hypoglycemia exposure wasn't randomized. Infants who developed it may differ from those who didn't in ways the analysis can't fully adjust for — severity of prematurity, birth weight, NICU course, socioeconomic factors affecting follow-up. Residual confounding is live. And school-age follow-up of neonatal cohorts has a known attrition problem — the follow-up rate is the number to check before citing this at a protocol committee.
JUNO [level]: But you're not walking away from the finding.
CASPAR [direct]: No. The original randomization gives this more structural weight than a de novo cohort study. The association is real. And you don't need causal proof to reinforce glucose monitoring — the rationale was already there; this paper gives it developmental stakes.
JUNO [pressing]: Two practice changes. What are they?
CASPAR [measured]: First, the antenatal counseling sentence. Before this paper, the trade-off was respiratory benefit against a metabolic risk that was acknowledged but not developmentally quantified. The sentence now is: low blood sugar in this population has been associated with worse developmental outcomes at school age, and we will monitor glucose closely after delivery. That is a different sentence than "steroids may cause low blood sugar."
JUNO [flat]: Those are not the same sentence.
CASPAR [continuing]: Second is the postnatal handoff. Late preterm infants post-steroid exposure already warrant glucose monitoring. This paper reinforces why the monitoring matters beyond the immediate clinical picture. The NICU team should know the steroid exposure history in that context.
JUNO [direct]: What doesn't the paper tell you?
CASPAR [honest]: The threshold and duration of hypoglycemia that matter most developmentally. Below 40 is the defined exposure, but whether 38 for two hours carries the same risk as 28 for six hours isn't answered here. Early intervention protocols — dextrose gel, aggressive early feeds — are not evaluated in this analysis. The association is characterized. The mitigation question is not.
JUNO [quiet]: A family who just received steroids for threatened late preterm delivery is being told there's a downstream metabolic risk that may not be fully preventable.
CASPAR [flat]: The data characterizes the risk. It doesn't tell you how to eliminate it.

CASPAR [brisk]: From BJOG, 2026: Barrett and colleagues, a systematic review and meta-analysis of 2.4 million women — hypertensive disorders of pregnancy and long-term risk of retinal and ophthalmic disease. Greater than three-fold increased risk of diabetic retinopathy and other ophthalmic disease in women with prior HDP. Larger magnitude than most of us are citing when we counsel these patients about long-term sequelae.
JUNO [noting]: Cardiac and renal sequelae are well-characterized. Ophthalmic isn't.
CASPAR [precise]: The caveat is heterogeneity — different HDP definitions across included studies, variable follow-up durations, different ascertainment methods. But the signal extends beyond women who develop overt diabetes, and ophthalmology referral should be on the long-term sequelae checklist. It isn't, at most centers.
JUNO [direct]: From AJOG MFM, 2026: Berghella and colleagues, a review synthesizing level-one evidence — whether vaginal progesterone and cerclage work through complementary pathways for short cervix. If the pathways are genuinely non-redundant, combination therapy in select patients is biologically defensible.
CASPAR [holding]: Review, not primary data. The mechanistic argument is plausible, the synthesis is careful — but the clinical evidence for additive benefit from combination therapy is incomplete. The biology is ahead of the trial.

CASPAR [confirming]: Correct. The question shifts from which one to whether both. The answer is pending.

CASPAR [precise]: Narrative synthesis, terminology standardization — not new outcome data, and the classification hasn't been prospectively validated.
